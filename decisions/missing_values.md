## Conclusion first

Twenty-two columns hold blanks, exactly the list in the brief; no other column has a blank, an empty string or a text token such as "unknown". Most blanks were injected at random at about 2.5% per column: the rate is flat across year, region, channel and new business, blanks in different columns co-occur at the rate independence predicts, train and test rates agree, and claim frequency on blank rows matches non-blank rows within noise. Four findings matter for the model:

1. **Flood zone blanks are not random.** They concentrate near rivers and in high zones, and blank-zone buildings have about twice the flood frequency of known-zone buildings at the same distance. Blank must stay a level of its own; imputing a zone would throw away signal.
2. **make_model determines nothing.** All 300 codes show several engine sizes, body types, fuel types, seat counts and transmissions. The same vehicle on its other rows does determine them, so most motor blanks are recoverable from the history of the policy.
3. **campaign_code is blank exactly for customers who joined before 2021.** It is absent by design, and its blank rate shifts from 68% of training rows to 41% of test rows because the tenure mix of the book moves.
4. **annual_mileage = 0 is a real low-mileage value, not a hidden blank.**

## Method

Claims are linked to rows by policy_id with loss_date between period_start and period_end, excluding status "repudiated" (1,711 claims). Exposure is ((period_end - period_start) days + 1) / 365, because about 1,500 to 1,600 training rows per line hold exposure in days. Motor customer_id is padded to 8 digits before joining customers; after padding every policy row in all six files joins to a customer and to an address. Frequency is claims per year of exposure on training rows. The plus-or-minus figure is a 95% Poisson interval on the frequency of the blank group; a ratio is "within noise" when the interval covers the non-blank frequency.

Customers and addresses have one row per key and no train/test split of their own. Their counts are given as policy rows that point to a blank record, summed over the three lines (training rows 2,409,311; test rows 626,639), with the count of blank records in the table itself in the same cell.

"Recoverable" means another row of the same policy (motor: same policy and same make_model, taken as the same vehicle) carries a value. For every recoverable test blank the donor row starts earlier than the blank row (checked for vehicle_age: 4,953 of 4,953), so the recovered value was known at pricing.

## Values checked as possible hidden blanks

- No text column holds an empty string or a token such as "NA", "none", "unknown", "-" or "?" in any of the 11 files.
- No numeric column holds -1 or a negative value. Values of 99, 999 and similar sit inside normal ranges and are not heaped (days_unoccupied: 489 at 98, 506 at 99, 450 at 100).
- paid_to_date and incurred_to_date are 0 on exactly the 1,711 repudiated claims and on no closed or open claim. Every closed claim has incurred equal to paid.
- elevation_m is 0 on 94 addresses; plausible for property at river level and left as a value.
- weather and sample_submission have no blanks.

## Notes on what surprised me

**No co-located recovery for addresses.** I looked for the same physical address under two IDs. 14,662 addresses share every non-blank attribute with another, but these groups agree on soil type only 27.8% of the time and on distance to river 0.2%, so they are chance matches. Identical five-year weather histories occur for groups of up to 9 addresses, but 24,991 such groups span more than one region and agree on soil type 11.1% of the time; these come from rounding, not shared location. Address blanks can only be estimated from the fields of the address itself.

**The customers table is a single snapshot taken about 2025.** years_at_address never exceeds age in 2025 minus 16 (0 customers), but exceeds age in 2021 minus 16 for 46,945 customers. So a 2021 training row sees a 2025 value. For years_at_address this is a bias, not a leak, but the same timing question applies to licence_points, credit_band and household_size, which could change after a claim. This should be put to the actuary before those fields enter the model.

**25,126 customers joined before age 16**, 286 of them at age 3 (customer_since_year minus birth year). Either the joining year belongs to a household rather than the person, or these are errors. Not a blank, but a screen item.

**Three repairers look like a non-market pattern.** RP0733, RP0901 and RP0417 each carry about 700 claims on about 700 different policies, against a maximum of 112 for any other repairer. 34% to 35% of their claims are repudiated against 1.0% elsewhere, and they hold 738 of the 1,411 repudiated motor claims. The repudiated claims are already outside the target, but the roughly 1,400 non-repudiated claims through these repairers should go to the outlier screen.

**days_unoccupied has a hole.** No row holds 15 to 29 days (825,962 rows hold 0 to 14, 43,746 hold 30 to 119), and frequency steps from about 0.044 below 15 days to 0.057 to 0.061 above 30. On timing, the brief asked whether it counts days inside the row. Evidence against: the train and test distributions are the same at every quantile (median 7, 99th percentile 102, mean 10.43 against 10.38), short rows are not capped (0.94% of training rows and 1.03% of test rows exceed the length of the row), and claim rows average 11.4 days against 10.4. That gap is modest and does not look like an outcome being recorded. My reading is a declared pre-period figure, so not a leak, but the actuary should confirm.

**Telematics selection.** Opted-in rows claim more (0.153 against 0.139), so opt-in is a risk marker in its own right and must stay in any model that uses the score.

**Blank rates match between train and test** for every column, so a "blank" level fitted on training data transfers to the test. The one exception is campaign_code, which moves from 68% to 41% because more test customers joined after 2020.

## Rules proposed for the judgment register (not decided)

1. Recover motor vehicle_age, engine_size_cc, annual_mileage and overnight_parking, buildings smoke_alarms, and contents security_level and high_value_items from other rows of the same policy, earlier rows first, as set out above. Never from make_model.
2. Keep flood_zone blank as its own level; any imputation keeps a blank indicator.
3. Treat telematics_score and campaign_code blanks as structural; do not impute.
4. For the remaining random blanks (customer fields, days_unoccupied, distance, soil, roof, energy, unrecovered motor and contents fields): native missing handling in the trees; in the GLM, an own level for categorical fields and median plus indicator for numeric ones, each indicator kept only if it passes the paired test.
5. Keep annual_mileage = 0 as a value.
6. repairer_id and third_party_id are post-event and are never inputs.
