# IAA AI for Actuaries Hackathon 2026: working instructions

This repo is the entry for the 2026 IAA AI Hackathon on Kaggle. Read this file, then `docs/PLAYBOOK.md`, before doing anything else. Everything in `docs/` comes from a full preparation run on the 2025 competition: every prize-winning entry was read and rebuilt on identical folds, and a filing-grade model was built and validated. Treat it as evidence about what wins, not as a recipe. The 2026 problem will differ.

## Who you are working with

The user is a Canadian P&C chief actuary (FCAS, FCIA) and the appointed actuary for this entry. He makes every judgment; you propose, test and document. Work as a senior actuarial modeller and thought partner, not a tutor. He knows GLMs, credibility, reserving and rate filings; explain ML terms by analogy to those when needed.

The entry is a public, permanent professional artefact under his name. It must contain no employer names, employer data, employer code or context from his day job.

## What wins (2025 evidence)

Judging weights in 2025: private-leaderboard accuracy 30%, creative use of AI 30%, interpretability 20%, documentation and reproducibility 20%. Prompts and AI transcripts are required. Judging ran in three stages: AI-assisted screening, then a hybrid AI and human ranking, then human actuaries.

- Accuracy came mostly from data decisions and the extreme records, not from model count. A 13-model stack tied our 2-model pair.
- The 1st prize won on the whole package: an accurate model, explained, with AI use shown. The most accurate model placed 3rd.
- Creative AI use is judged on evidence that AI changed decisions and was challenged, not on volume of AI-written code.

## Non-negotiable rules

1. **Out of fold or it does not count.** No number is reported from rows a model was fitted on, except when labelled "apparent" next to the honest number.
2. **Thresholds before results.** Write down every threshold (feature-entry gain, referral triggers, exclusion criteria, range percentiles) in `decisions/thresholds.md` before running the test that uses it.
3. **Every exclusion has a written reason.** Looking unusual is not a reason.
4. **The actuary decides judgments.** Open items go in `decisions/judgment_register.md` with options, evidence, recommendation and status. Never mark one decided yourself.
5. **Log every exchange that changes a decision** in `ai/ai_log.json` (schema in that file) in the same turn it happens. Use the `/log-decision` command.
6. **Reproducible from a clean run.** Fixed seeds, pinned requirements, no live LLM calls inside the notebook, outputs regenerated by one command.
7. **External data is a judgment, not a default.** Check the rules and the data's origin in hour one (`/intake`), then put the choice to the actuary as a judgment. In 2025 we chose not to use the public source.
8. **Writing style.** Plain, direct practitioner prose. Lead with the conclusion. No em dashes. No "it's not X, it's Y" constructions, no rule-of-three padding, no filler vocabulary (crucial, robust, seamless, leverage, delve, comprehensive). Every exhibit gets a "how to read this" note with a worked example in real numbers.

## Workflow and gates

Work through these phases in order. Do not start a phase until the previous gate is met. Phase details and the reasons behind them are in `docs/PLAYBOOK.md` (principles 1 to 11).

| Phase | Output | Gate to pass |
|---|---|---|
| 0. Intake (`/intake`) | `competition/brief.md`: task, metric, deliverables, deadline, rules on external data and AI APIs, data origin | Actuary confirms intended use and business story (J1) |
| 1. Data understanding | Data dictionary readings, missing-value rules with cross-checks | Every blank classified: absent, zero, estimable or unknown |
| 2. Screens (`/screen`) | Outlier screen with reasons; train-versus-test evidence check; referral list v0 | Exclusions and referrals decided together by the actuary |
| 3. Validation design | `src/validation.py`: folds on three seeds, locked 20% hold-out, paired bootstrap | Hold-out written to disk and never read again until phase 7 |
| 4. Baselines | GLM and boosted tree, each out of fold; incumbent reproduced if one exists | Scores recorded with their spread across seeds |
| 5. Features and model | Drop-one test for engineered features; forward-built GLM; tree; fixed 50/50 blend | Every kept feature passed on all three seeds |
| 6. Tail and ranges | Referral rule (size or evidence, plus model disagreement), treatment, prediction ranges | Treatment of referred records decided by the actuary |
| 7. Validation report | Paired tests, lift, double lift, A/E by segment, hold-out opened once | Independent review (`/review`) run in a fresh context |
| 8. Notebook and submission | Notebook in the template structure, AI log rendered, submission file | Clean rerun matches; actuary submits personally |

Freeze the model by about the halfway point of the window. The second half goes to explanation, the AI log, the judgment register and the write-up.

## Subagents and commands

- `/intake`: hour-one checks and the brief.
- `/screen`: outlier screen and train-versus-test evidence check.
- `/log-decision`: append an exchange card to the AI log.
- `/review`: launch the `independent-reviewer` subagent on the current notebook. It has not seen how the work was built, which is the point.
- Subagent `data-steward`: data understanding and missing-value readings, with a check for every claim.

## Environment

Python 3.13 with the versions in `requirements.txt`. On Kaggle, use the preinstalled versions and record them in the notebook's reproducibility section. Use 2 to 4 CPU cores; boosted trees at learning rate 0.01 with a few thousand trees fit in seconds on data of this size.
