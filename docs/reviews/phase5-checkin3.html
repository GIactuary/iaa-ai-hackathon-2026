How every test on this page reads. Each challenger is fitted on the same W2 rating factors as the line GLM and scored out of time. Scoring A fits 2021 to 2022 and scores 2023; scoring B fits 2021 to 2023 and scores 2024. "Saved" is the drop in total Tweedie deviance on the scored year against the line GLM, with every line held at its actual total, so only ranking counts; a negative number means the challenger ranks worse. The keep rule needs a saving in both scorings and a 95% customer-bootstrap interval on A that lies above zero. Example: frequency × severity in motor saves 12,541 on A, interval −12,076 to 34,902, and 35,947 on B. The interval crosses zero, so it fails.

Step 6 · structure (P3) and the excess factor (K2)

Four structures on the same rating factors

Every model uses the W2 rating factors of its line, unchanged; only the structure differs. The design was written and committed before anything was fitted (decisions/phase5_step6_design.md).

Each is scored as it would be priced: an off-balance to the weather-normalised indication (one per line for S1, one per peril for S2 to S4), times the scored year's weather. That gives the per-peril structures their textbook advantage: bodily injury's 2023 step reaches each row in proportion to its own injury share. The bare form, without the level chain, gives the same verdicts.

Result

No per-peril structure passes; motor frequency × severity comes closest

Challenger against S1LineA: savedA: 95% intervalB: savedPasses

How to read this. Each row replaces the line GLM with the challenger. In motor, frequency × severity lowers deviance by 12,541 on 2023 and 35,947 on 2024, and motor ranking D² on 2024 moves from 6.35% to 6.50%; but the 2023 interval runs from −12,076 to 34,902, so it fails. In buildings the same structure costs 19,093 on 2023 and 33,017 on 2024.

Why property loses. Splitting by peril divides the claims. Buildings fire, subsidence and flood each carry about 200 to 500 claims a year, against the same set of rating factors in each peril's GLM; motor perils carry about 1,000 to 11,000. Of the per-peril forms, frequency × severity beats a Tweedie per peril across lines: +63,293 on 2023 (interval 3,445 to 127,129), +29,888 on 2024. Severity relativities matter: removing them (S4 against S3) costs 143,157 in buildings and 146,691 in contents on 2023. Sum insured drives cost per claim in property. The per-peril level, the textbook advantage, is noise here: in motor it moves frequency × severity by +2,940 on 2023 and −2,799 on 2024 against the bare form.

The excess factor (K2 retest, C2)

Spreading the large-loss load over the line ranks better

Above-cap cost sits in a few perils. Final-fit factors: motor bodily injury 1.091 against the line's 1.031; buildings fire 1.087 and flood 1.011 against 1.022; contents flood 1.073 against 1.018. Every other peril is at 1.000 or within 0.002 of it.

Per-peril factor against line factorLineA: savedA: 95% intervalB: savedPasses

How to read this. Bodily injury is about 34% of motor capped cost, so the line's 3.1% load equals a 9.1% load on injury and nothing on other perils. Moved onto injury alone, the load loses 4,205 on 2023 (interval −6,753 to −1,404): which policies produce the few claims above 100k is not predicted by their injury share. Only contents passes, inside a structure that itself loses. S1 has no peril split, so it takes the line factor in any case.

The 2025 weather row by row is inside the noise, and slightly worse

How to read this. K4 multiplies each row by its own year's weather. On the line GLM that is the only difference between the priced and bare forms, so this row tests it. In buildings it costs 4,229 on 2023 (interval −18,715 to 11,375) and 7,438 on 2024. The level is untouched either way: the 2025 net effect is -0.04% motor, +0.06% buildings and -0.28% contents.

Added after seeing results, and one diagnostic set aside This weather test was not in the design. I added it after the scored frames showed the gap between the bare and priced line GLM, and it is reported as information for card M3, not as a pre-registered test. The design's per-peril diagnostic, which scores each peril's component against that peril's own cost, turned out to be uninformative. In motor it is decided by rows whose cover excludes the peril: the Tweedie per peril drives third-party-only rows to near-zero own-damage, glass and theft predictions, which wins a single-peril score but barely moves the line total. It stays in the evidence file and decides nothing.
