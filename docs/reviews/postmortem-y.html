How the tests read. Each test refits on 2021 to 2022 and scores 2023 (A), and refits on 2021 to 2023 and scores 2024 (B), on customers outside the hold-out, exactly as every Part 1 decision was tested. "Saved" is the drop in total Tweedie deviance against the reference at a common level per line; the keep rule is unchanged (better in both years, and the 2023 interval above zero). The GLM tests are against the W2 GLM; the tree tests are against LightGBM on the Part 1 tree inputs. Example: in buildings, the GLM plus the premium as our fields predict it saves 110,298 on 2023, interval 62,977 to 164,210, and 123,815 on 2024, so it passes.

What the tests found

The premium's gain is in the part our fields predict

TestLine2023: saved2023: 95% interval2024: savedPasses

How to read this. The premium was split in two before it went near a claim. A model fitted only on our own rating fields explains 97.5% of the buildings premium (largest inputs: flood_zone, sum_insured, region, distance_to_river_km); that is "the premium as our fields predict it". What is left over is "the remainder". The first carries the whole gain and the second none, so the premium holds no hidden or post-event information, and nothing from a field we exclude (such as the P6 marketing fields) can reach the model through it. Wider tree inputs pass across lines (+150,967 on 2023) but in no line on its own.

How to read this. Rows are sorted by how far the premium-informed GLM moves the W2 GLM's price and cut into tenths. In 2023, in the tenth it raises most (by 22% on average), actual cost was 1.28 times the W2 GLM's price and 0.98 times the premium-informed price: the GLM under-priced these homes, which sit 1.85 km from a river (median) against 2.37 for the book, and lose 44% of their cost to flood against 28%. Flood claims are rare, so four years of our own experience could not support how zone, river distance and elevation combine; the trees had both fields and still did not learn it. The current rating plan carries it with more credibility.

How to read this. The host confirmed that recoveries are recorded only when a claim settles. Settled own damage claims recover 23.6% of incurred, theft 4.0%; no other peril recovers. Applying those rates to claims still open at 31 December 2024 takes 1.36M off accident year 2024. The 2025 motor indication falls 1.28%, more than 2024's share alone suggests, because the overstated 2024 severity also steepens the own damage trend. Buildings and contents do not change.

Where I was wrong (D31) Before testing, I ranked missing address weather as the second likely cause of the gap; it tested at −62,438 on 2023 and −5,623 on 2024, nothing. My first wide-input test also let the trees split on make-model, a 300-level code Part 1 had already shown to be noise (M7), and motor lost 414,287. The correction was written down and committed before the rerun; the numbers above are from the corrected test.
