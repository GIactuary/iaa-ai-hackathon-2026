How every test on this page reads. Each family's model is fitted on earlier years and scored on a later one, against the W2 GLM. Scoring A fits 2021 to 2022 and scores 2023; scoring B fits 2021 to 2023 and scores 2024. "Saved" is the drop in total Tweedie deviance on the scored year, with every line held at its actual total, so only ranking counts; a negative number means the model ranks worse than the GLM. The keep rule needs a saving in both scorings and a 95% customer-bootstrap interval on A above zero. Example: the spline GLM in contents saves 13,776 on A, interval −939 to 27,773, and 15,576 on B. The interval crosses zero, so it fails.

Step 7 · model families (P7, C5)

Four families, each tuned on 2021 to 2022 only

Each family was tuned on folds of 2021 and 2022, including the training power from 1.1 to 1.9, and its strongest version chosen there. Only then was it fitted to the earlier years and scored on 2023 and 2024 (decisions/phase5_step7_design.md).

Your suggestion · standardised inputs (D21)

Standardising made the penalised GLM work in contents

What it did. With every input scaled to the same spread, the tuning chose a strong penalty in contents that drops weak levels outright; unscaled, it chose almost none. On 2024 the standardised GLM ranks 50,180 better than the unscaled one (+3,392 against −46,788, both against the W2 GLM), and its spline version is the penalised-GLM pick in contents.

Where I was wrong (logged as D23 and D22) For motor frequency × severity in the penalised GLM, I reused the penalty tuned for the one-model-per-line version. On the frequency and severity scales the same penalty is far stronger, so it flattened almost every relativity and the test lost 1,466,913. That test is set aside; step 6's unpenalised test is the GLM evidence on this structure. Building the interim file, I found that my phase 1 rule had left two contents blank levels too thin to estimate, priced near zero in the 18 October baseline. You chose to price them at the average of the known levels (P8-R2).
