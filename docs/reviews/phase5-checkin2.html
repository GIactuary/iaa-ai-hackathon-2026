How every test on this page reads. Each row adds something to the model and scores it out of time. Scoring A fits 2021 to 2022 and scores 2023; scoring B fits 2021 to 2023 and scores 2024. "Saved" is the drop in total Tweedie deviance on the scored year, with every line held at its actual total, so only ranking counts. The keep rule needs a saving in both scorings and a 95% customer-bootstrap interval on A that lies above zero. Example: own-policy history in motor saves 77,289 on A, interval 41,027 to 115,031, and 103,319 on B, so it is kept.

Step 4 · claims history (C7)

A credibility-weighted experience mod

Each row sees only claims reported before it starts, or before 1 January of the pricing year if it starts later, which mirrors pricing 2025 with data to 31 December 2024. Hold-out customers are removed from every count. The history enters each line's GLM as one term:

relativity = modβ,   mod = (A + k) ÷ (E + k) = Z·A/E + (1 − Z),   Z = E ÷ (E + k)
AClaims on the policy's earlier rows, reported before the cutoff
EClaims expected over the same exposure: class frequency × exposure × share reported by the cutoff
kBühlmann-Straub constant, estimated from the fitting years (card L1)
βFitted by the GLM; 1 is pure experience rating on top of the class rate

How to read this. A motor policy with three years of history at an average class rate expects 0.44 claims. With k = 2.76, its history earns Z = 0.44 ÷ 3.20 = 13.8% credibility. Claim-free, its mod is 2.76 ÷ 3.20 = 0.863, and the GLM applies 0.8630.86 = 0.880, a 12% discount. One claim makes the mod 3.76 ÷ 3.20 = 1.175, applied as 1.149. Property policies expect fewer claims, so the same history earns less credibility, but one claim weighs more against the small expectation. The E values are the 75th percentile of the 2025 test rows; the median is lower.

How to read this. Negative means the form loses to the credibility mod. Raw counts (C7 option c, tested as evidence) lose 172,685 on A. A count learned on one-year histories in 2022 overstates the same count on two-year histories in 2023, and credibility corrects that by construction. Holding β at 1 costs little and is not significantly worse.

Where I was wrong in this step (logged as D17) I designed k as the non-parametric Bühlmann-Straub estimate, with the Poisson-gamma moment estimate only as a check. Run 1 made k infinite for buildings on scoring A, which nulled the history there, and contents k swung from 4.6 to 92.7. The reason: a property policy-year expects about 0.05 claims, so the non-parametric between-risk estimate is mostly Poisson noise. The process variance is Poisson (1.02 to 1.06; 1.004 claims per incident), and the Poisson-gamma estimate was steady: motor 2.35 to 2.76, buildings 1.77 to 2.30, contents 2.08 to 2.54. In run 1 the GLM's own slopes implied k between 3.0 and 3.5 in six of eight fits (contents gave 4.1 and 6.0), much nearer the Poisson-gamma values than the non-parametric ones. I wrote the amendment down before run 2 was scored, and both runs are kept.

Worth knowing. Household size, marital status and occupation all fail, so no fairness judgment is needed on them now. Days unoccupied fails too, so its point-of-use question is moot. The flood measures in buildings save 87,486 to 95,241 on 2024 but lose on 2023, where the flood-score interval runs from -175,577 to 28,891: flood cost is too lumpy to rank on two years. Years licensed in motor saves about 25,000 in both years and misses only on the interval.

Where I was wrong again (to log as D18) The make-model encoding (M7) used the same non-parametric credibility estimate, now on capped cost by model. It found no between-model variance (k infinite), so the encoding was 1 everywhere and the test was empty. I checked by hand with the efficient estimator on claim counts: across 300 models, the spread of actual over expected claims is 0.085 against 0.079 from noise alone, so make-model adds at most about 3% in frequency beyond V1's vehicle factors, and nothing detectable in cost. M7 is a true null on this data, but the empty test was my design's fault, and I should have caught it after step 4.
