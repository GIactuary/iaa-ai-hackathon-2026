A technical price built with AI and decided by an actuary

IAA AI-Assisted Hackathon 2026, Parts 1 and 2. Bavan Amirthalingam, FCAS, FCIA. Code, evidence files, decision briefs and the AI log: https://github.com/GIactuary/iaa-ai-hackathon-2026. Every number below comes from a committed evidence file in outputs/ or a decision note in decisions/; Part 2's exhibits are generated by scripts/exhibits_part2.py. Numbers are out of fold unless labelled apparent.

Summary

We priced 2025 motor, home buildings and home contents twice: for an insurer in a developed market with four years of data (Part 1), then for an emerging market with two years and a 2025 book that is 76% new business from an acquired broker portfolio (Part 2). Every candidate change was tested against one rule, written and committed before the test ran: it must lower deviance on a later year out of time and in cross-validation, with a 95% customer-bootstrap interval above zero. Where the rule could not decide, the actuary did, and those calls are reported as his.

Part 1 finished 15th on the private leaderboard at 0.13641 (first 0.13923; places 5 to 20 lie within 0.0019). A blend of model families per line, set to a weather-normalised 2025 level, beat our working GLM on customers locked away until the end. Part 2 borrows Part 1's risk shape, fitted on a million developed-market rows, and lets the emerging data set the level and add only what it proves: credibility applied to a whole rating plan. On customers locked away until the end, the emerging additions held in motor and lost in buildings, so buildings went back to Part 1's shape alone. Three of four independent AI reviews then found that our own line-by-line rule, applied consistently, also favoured pricing motor by peril. The final file scores 0.15712 on the public leaderboard, against 0.14266 for our first check file.

The actuary decided every judgment on briefs that set out options, evidence and a recommendation. 52 exchanges are logged, including those where the AI was wrong. In Part 1 he proposed the level method himself; in Part 2 he overruled the AI's recommendation on the credibility standard for new business, then returned to it after a board reading.

1. Part 1: a technical price for an established book

Use and data. The price is a technical risk premium, the expected ultimate cost of each 2025 row, to set rates, measure adequacy against the current premium and refer risks the evidence cannot support (J1). Buildings ran at a loss ratio of 1.29 to 1.36 in every year from 2021 to 2024. The data is synthetic: 2.4 million policy rows, 163,150 claims valued at 31 December 2024, 626,639 rows to price. The target had to be built: net cost by loss date, repudiated claims out, claims capped (100k motor, 200k buildings, 30k contents), and each row's exposure scaled by its expected reporting completion, which acts as IBNR at row level. Stated data rules repair the planted errors on training and test rows alike (sum insured entered 1,000 times too large on 2,797 training and 730 test rows, for example). No record was excluded; an 11.9M bodily injury reserve was investigated and kept as a genuine large loss (X1).

Validation. Every choice was judged as a 2025 price is set: fit 2021 to 2022 and score 2023 (A), fit 2021 to 2023 and score 2024 (B), on the competition's Tweedie deviance at power 1.5 after each line is rescaled to its actual total, as rating plans are compared after an off-balance. A random 15% of customers (73,227) was locked away before any fit and opened once, after the freeze.

How to read this. Ranking D² is the share of a flat rate's deviance the model removes. The blend's +92,357 is 0.18% of the W2 GLM's 2023 deviance: small, because most deviance is the chance of who claims, but its interval sits above zero and it won in all 2,000 customer resamples.

Of 67 engineered features three stayed; climate normals and plain gradient boosting (−242,328 on 2023) lost to the GLM, because the data behaves like a multiplicative rating plan. Claims history is experience rating: each policy's mod is (A + k) ÷ (E + k), A its prior claims and E its class's expected claims, entered with a fitted slope of 0.86 on the log mod. In motor k = 2.76, so a policy expecting 0.37 claims over three years carries ((1 + 2.76) ÷ (0.37 + 2.76))^0.86 = 1.17 after one claim and 0.90 claim-free. The families (GLM, an explainable additive model, boosted corrections to the GLM, CatBoost) were tuned on inner folds and weighted per line on one scoring, tested on the other.

Level. The 2025 indication is built peril by peril from weather-normalised experience, the method the actuary proposed when the AI's own recommendation turned out to rest on a bug (D16): frequency restated to normal weather, trends fitted on the restated years within −5% to +12% a year, then the realised 2025 weather. A motor row the GLM rates at 500 CU at the 2024 level is priced at 500 × 1.058 (cost above the cap) × 1.009 (to the 2025 indication) × 0.9996 (2025 weather) = 533.5 CU. Expected 2025 cost over current premium: motor 0.95, buildings 1.43, contents 0.83.

2. Part 2: the same pipeline in an emerging market

What changed. Two years (2023, 2024), six regions, ages in bands, no credit score, mileage or telematics, occupation typed by the customer, local construction codes, and gender, which this market allows in pricing. The training book is 86% renewals; the 2025 book is 76% new business. Part 1's code ran unchanged with a market configuration; its data rules stopped on the new construction codes until a map was written, and two rules were added: open claims net of expected recoveries (R10) and occupation cleaned (R11).

Borrow the shape, learn the level (P2-C (b)). Part 1's relativities beat a GLM fitted on emerging data in every line, out of time and in cross-validation (capped cost, cross-validation, all lines: +351,073 [252,585, 467,729]). The developed market is the complement; the emerging data earns weight only where it shows it.

The test (P2-V), written before the first candidate. A change is kept if it lowers deviance out of time (fit 2023, score 2024) and in customer-grouped cross-validation on three seeds, with the cross-validation interval from 2,000 customer resamples above zero. 15% of customers were locked away first. Rounds 1 to 3 judged that interval pooled over the three lines, with one exception: the premium term failed pooled and was re-tested in motor alone, a line chosen after its per-line gains were seen. From the hold-out on, every line was judged on its own. The test can confirm a gain of about 0.002 of D² in motor and 0.004 in buildings (register WU), so "failed" often means "not proven".

Level and new business. A 2025 indication per peril at normal weather with Part 1's trends, excess factors (motor 1.007 from its own large claims, property from Part 1's shares), then each row's 2025 weather. New business takes a credibility-weighted factor: new against renewal, actual over model, moved toward the emerging figure by Z = √(n ÷ 1,082), the claim-count standard. Motor: 697 claims, Z 0.80, factor 1.114. Two reviews found that a ratio of cost calls for the pure-premium standard, 1,082 × (1 + CV²) (motor Z 0.35, factor 1.050), and our validation cannot separate the two. The actuary first chose the pure-premium standard against the AI's recommendation, then returned to the count standard when both readings on the 2025 book favoured the larger factor (public 0.15712 against 0.15657, a difference inside the board's noise; H-NS, D49). Expected 2025 cost over written premium: motor 0.96, buildings 1.20, contents 0.77. Ten rows are referred for underwriting review at the model price (H-R (a)).

From risk premium to office premium (Appendix A). Annual basis, for each of the 20 reference policies: (risk premium + catastrophe load at 12% of property risk premium + 85 CU) ÷ (1 − 15% variable − commission (broker 20%, direct 0%) − 8% tax − 8% profit) (OP-T, OP-B). Buildings B00229821, broker: 359.0 + 43.1 + 85 = 487.1 CU, ÷ 0.49 = 994.0 CU, against 285.6 CU charged today. On risk premium alone, before any expense, 12 of the 20 already cost more than they are charged, including all 7 buildings policies and no contents policy. The expense table drives much of the rest: a broker policy needs 2.04 times its risk premium plus 173 CU (2.29 times on property, with the cat load). The acquired broker book is priced well below its technical premium. No reference policy is sold through a comparison site, whose commission the table does not give (P2-E (d)).

Gender (Appendix B). It enters motor only, as one factor fitted on emerging rows on top of a shape that has none (Part 1's data carries no gender). Without it, men's actual cost runs 1.136 times what the Part 1 shape expects and women's 0.865. With it, a man pays 1.30 times what an otherwise identical woman pays (95% interval 1.15 to 1.48); removing gender at the same motor total would lower men's prices by 11.6% and raise women's by 15.3%.

Limitations. The public leader (0.17353 when last read) is 0.016 of D² ahead of our final file. At the board's size two equally good but different models differ by a standard deviation of about 0.004 (register WU), so most of that gap is real, and our tests could not find where it comes from; the level is not the cause, since one line off by 10% costs under 0.001. The new-business factor is the largest judgment in the 2025 level; it is measured on the insurer's own new business and applied to an acquired broker book, where the raw motor new-business frequency ratio is lower for broker business (1.32 [1.15, 1.52]) than for direct (1.67). Buildings rests on twelve fires and Part 1's shape and excess load. Realised 2025 weather is used.

3. Use of AI, and the guardrails around it

AI worked in roles set by standing prompts (data steward, modeller, filing writer, independent reviewer; ai/prompt_library.md). Each decision went to the actuary as a brief with options, evidence and a recommendation; he decided, challenged or proposed his own method. Every exchange that changed a decision is a card in ai/ai_log.json: 52 cards with the question, the AI's answer, the actuary's challenge where there was one, the test, the result and the decision.

Guardrails. Every test design is committed before the test runs, and every threshold is dated before use (decisions/thresholds*.md; the code refuses undated ones). Each hold-out was opened once to judge the frozen model on a written rule; in Part 2, later candidates were also scored on it and every choice made after opening is reported as such. The public board decided one choice on a rule written before upload (new business, M2-B); the actuary also uploaded an extra variant and made the final call on the credibility standard after a board reading, both disclosed. No record was excluded. Gender is shown with its effect and the price without it. Both final files rebuild from the raw data with one command each (scripts/reproduce.py), run in an empty copy of the repository, and match the uploaded files byte for byte.
